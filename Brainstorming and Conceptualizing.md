# Brainstorming and Conceptualizing

## Project

- Name: Pain Olympics
- Working description: A dark-humor competitive card game about comparing trauma, arguing your case, and winning rounds through card play, table reaction, and optional judging.
- Current status: Early concept exploration

## Process

- We are documenting one question at a time.
- Each answer will be added here before moving to the next question.
- The goal is to shape the game's tone, rules, audience, modes, and implementation plan.

## Answer Log

### Question 1

- Status: Answered
- Question: Are players only supposed to play cards that are true for them, or can they bluff, roleplay, or use shared deck cards that are not literally autobiographical?
- Answer: The game should lean toward decks that are customized to the individual player, so cards generally reflect things that are true for that person. There should still be some room for bluffing, exaggeration, or persuasive play, with a `call bullshit` style mechanic if another player thinks a card does not really apply.
- Working takeaway: Personalized decks feel central to the concept. The challenge system may become one of the core mechanics because it supports both authenticity and table drama.

### Question 2

- Status: Answered
- Question: When a round is decided, should the winner be the player with the objectively more severe trauma card, the player who argues their card best, or the player whose card best fits the prompt?
- Answer: By default, the round should go to the player who plays the more objectively traumatizing card. There should also be a challenge feature where both players can state their case, plus a `bullshit` button if someone thinks the other player is lying or stretching the truth. If challenged, a player can elaborate on why the card is valid for them, or keep trying to sell it. If the dispute is not resolved, both players play a follow-up card and the winner is decided by the combined preset point value of the two-card hand. If a third player is present as a witness or judge, they can decide the winner directly or choose to trigger a tiebreaker.
- Working takeaway: The core loop is severity-first, but social arbitration is a major part of the game. Preset point values work best as tiebreak support and judge assistance rather than the only deciding factor.

### Question 3

- Status: Answered
- Question: What do you want the overall feel of the game to be: more cathartic and self-aware, more ruthless and chaotic like a party game, or a deliberate mix of both?
- Answer: The game should be a thorough mix of both. It should feel cathartic and self-aware, but also ruthless and chaotic when the situation calls for it. The preferred tone is gallows humor rather than executioner humor.
- Working takeaway: The humor should come from people surviving or processing pain, not from cruelty toward people who are suffering. That distinction should guide writing, art direction, moderation, and how cards are categorized or excluded.

### Question 4

- Status: Answered
- Question: Are there any topics you want excluded from the core game, softened, or moved into optional decks only?
- Answer: At this stage, there are no hard limits. If a card reflects a real experience a player has had, they should be able to play it. More sensitive or intense material can be separated into optional decks instead of being excluded entirely.
- Working takeaway: The project is currently aiming for broad expressive range, with deck segmentation as the main safety and tone-management tool rather than outright content bans.

### Question 5

- Status: Answered
- Question: Do you want the cards themselves to read more like short phrases and labels, or more like fully written punchlines with a distinct comedic voice?
- Answer: A mix of both. Short phrases and labels as well as fully written punchlines with a distinct comedic voice should both have their place. This also connects to having two different modes over time: an MVP or early-build `war` mode where players simply play cards against one another, and a later `prompt` mode where cards are played alongside Cards Against Humanity style prompts. Different styles of card writing may suit different modes or situations.
- Working takeaway: The game likely needs a flexible content system rather than one rigid card format. `War` mode can support more direct or declarative cards, while `prompt` mode may benefit from punchier phrasing and stronger setup-payoff writing.

### Question 6

- Status: Answered
- Question: Is your priority to make this mainly a physical card game, mainly a digital portfolio project, or something designed for both from the start?
- Answer: The priority is to start as a digital portfolio project. If it goes well, it could eventually be hosted on platforms like itch.io and similar sites. A physical adaptation would be a later-stage goal, ideally if the game gains enough traction to justify making it profitable.
- Working takeaway: Product planning should optimize for a digital MVP first. Systems like deck storage, solo play, UI, and content management should be built with a browser-based experience in mind, while keeping the rules portable enough for a future physical version.

### Question 7

- Status: Answered
- Question: How do you imagine personalized decks being built: by answering onboarding questions, selecting tags or life experiences from a list, writing custom cards, or some combination of those?
- Answer: Personalized decks should start with onboarding questions that surface a relevant set of cards for the player. After that, players should be able to browse lists or search the full available card pool. Users should also be able to submit ideas for custom cards, with approval or denial handled by the creator.
- Working takeaway: The content pipeline likely needs three layers: guided onboarding for initial personalization, a searchable library for manual refinement, and a moderated submission system for expanding the card pool over time.

### Question 8

- Status: Answered
- Question: For solo mode, what feels most exciting to you right now: funny NPC opponents, a tournament ladder, a story or career mode, or some mix of those?
- Answer: The most exciting direction right now is a mix centered on funny NPC opponents and tournament-style progression. Possible opponents could be exaggerated archetypes or themed characters such as different kinds of `Karen` characters, authority figures, historical figures, presidents, and eventually even an `AI` character with its own joke-card pool based on artificial-intelligence struggles. The overall idea is to blend humorous opponent design with an ongoing solo tournament structure.
- Working takeaway: Solo mode should likely start with authored NPC personalities and a tournament loop, with each opponent potentially having themed decks, banter, and special rules. This gives the game personality without needing fully dynamic AI to make solo play interesting.

### Question 9

- Status: Answered
- Question: Should preset point values be visible on cards, hidden behind the scenes, or only used in tie situations and solo-mode logic?
- Answer: Preset point values should be visible on the cards so players can intuitively understand the value of what they are holding and make informed choices during play.
- Working takeaway: Visible values support strategic decision-making and make the game easier to learn. The challenge will be making sure visible scoring does not flatten the social or comedic parts of play too much.

### Question 10

- Status: Answered
- Question: Are you attached to `Pain Olympics` as the public title, or do you want to keep it as a working title while we explore alternatives for release?
- Answer: `Pain Olympics` is a title the creator likes a lot and currently sees as a solid public-facing name.
- Working takeaway: The current title has strong creative appeal and brand identity. If release or platform constraints come up later, title alternatives can still be explored, but the default assumption should be that `Pain Olympics` remains the main name.

### Question 11

- Status: Answered
- Question: Who do you picture as the ideal player and table for this game: close friends with shared trust and dark humor, internet strangers, a broad party-game audience, streamers, or some other core audience?
- Answer: Both feel valid, but the game will likely start out best suited for closer friends with shared trust and dark humor. As the concept gets built out and refined, the long-term goal is for it to become more of a broad-audience game.
- Working takeaway: The MVP can assume a safer, more trusted social context, which lowers early moderation complexity. If the game grows toward a broader audience later, onboarding, content warnings, matchmaking, and safety controls may need to become more intentional.

### Question 12

- Status: Answered
- Question: For the first playable digital version, do you imagine this being local pass-and-play, same-screen multiplayer, private online matches with friends, or open online matchmaking?
- Answer: The preference is for online play with matchmaking-style play in the first digital version.
- Working takeaway: The long-term multiplayer vision leans toward online connectivity rather than only local play. For the MVP, we may still decide to stage this carefully, but the product direction points toward online match flow rather than just pass-and-play.

### Question 13

- Status: Answered
- Question: For the MVP platform, do you want to start with a browser-based web app, a mobile app, or a responsive web app that works on both phones and desktop?
- Answer: The goal is to build out all of the above rather than committing to only one platform family.
- Working takeaway: The product should be planned with cross-platform reach in mind from the start. In practice, this likely points toward a responsive web-first approach or architecture that can later support browser and app-style experiences without rewriting the whole game.

### Question 14

- Status: Answered
- Question: How private should a player’s personalized trauma deck be: should other players only see cards once they’re played, or should profiles, deck themes, and card history be more visible?
- Answer: Decks and hands should only be visible to the player who holds them. Other players should not be able to see another player's hand unless a card is actually played.
- Working takeaway: Private hands are the default expectation. This supports strategy, reduces overexposure of personal material, and gives the digital version a clear information model for multiplayer UX.

### Question 15

- Status: Answered
- Question: During a challenge or `bullshit` call, how much explanation should a player be expected to give: a quick one-line defense, a short story, or whatever they feel comfortable sharing?
- Answer: Players should share only whatever they feel comfortable sharing.
- Working takeaway: The challenge system should invite explanation without requiring disclosure. That suggests flexible UI prompts, optional elaboration, and social rules that respect boundaries instead of forcing performance.

### Question 16

- Status: Answered
- Question: What should decide the overall winner of a match: first to a target score, best out of a set number of rounds, running out of cards, tournament brackets, or some combination of those?
- Answer: The winner should be the player with the highest score after a set number of rounds. When one card beats another, the winning player should gain not only the points from the card they played, but also the point value of the opponent's card.
- Working takeaway: Match flow should be round-based with cumulative scoring. The `card capture` scoring idea makes every win feel bigger and adds more weight to high-value clashes, which could create strong swing moments during a match.

### Question 17

- Status: Answered
- Question: Should players be able to use `challenge` and `bullshit` calls as often as they want, or should those be limited resources per match or per round?
- Answer: Each player should get 3 `challenge` uses and 3 `bullshit` uses.
- Working takeaway: Social dispute mechanics are limited resources, which helps keep rounds moving and makes players choose their interventions more strategically.

## Current Design Direction Notes

- Visual direction under consideration: a hybrid of minimalist party-card readability and premium playing-card framing.
- Working idea: keep the text-heavy bluntness needed for humor, but present cards with stronger borders, corner markers, hierarchy, and physical-card presence.
- Potential separation:
  - `War` cards could look more like playable deck cards.
  - Future `Prompt` cards could lean more toward the stark black-card / white-card contrast of party-card games.

### Question 18

- Status: Answered
- Question: For `War` mode trauma cards, do you want the main face of the card to feel more like a black-and-white CAH-style joke card, more like an ivory playing card with decorative borders and corner values, or a hybrid where the text area stays minimal but the frame feels like a real deck card?
- Answer: For now, the visual direction will follow a hybrid approach. `War` mode cards should keep the text readable and blunt, but gain stronger physical-card framing, corner values, and a more premium deck feel. Adding illustrations or pictures to cards is a possible later-stage feature, not a current priority.
- Working takeaway: The current UI pass should move toward a hybrid card treatment rather than either pure CAH minimalism or ornate traditional deck styling. Future art support should be considered in the layout, but not required yet.

### Question 19

- Answer: The first AI or computer opponent deck should be a mix of `overworked digital servant` and `passive-aggressive machine intelligence`.
- Working takeaway: The AI opponent should feel exhausted, useful, slightly bitter, and self-aware rather than purely robotic or purely goofy. Its deck voice can blend service-worker misery with machine sarcasm.

### Question 20

- Working takeaway: The AI/computer lane should become a family of machine-themed opponents rather than one generic bot. The first deck can still be a shared `computer deck` foundation, but it should be extensible into multiple personalities later.

### Question 22

- Status: Answered
- Question: For the first real board-layout pass, do you want the match screen to feel more like `opponent at the top, arena in the center, your hand at the bottom` or more like `side-by-side dueling panels with a central clash zone`?
- Answer: The preferred direction is `opponent at the top, arena in the center, your hand at the bottom`, with additional inspiration pulled from Pokemon-style battle presentation.
- Working takeaway: The match screen should shift away from side-by-side dashboard panels and toward a staged battle layout. The player's hand and controls should live at the bottom, the opponent presence should live at the top, and the central play area should feel like the battlefield.

### Question 23

- Status: Answered
